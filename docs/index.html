Inquisitive Conversational Agents: Foundations and Methods for Agent-Driven Strategic Questioning

Half-Day Tutorial

Tutorial Outline

While many conversational systems are designed to respond to the user, some settings require the agent to take control of the conversation and ask the questions. This interaction arises across domains: in medical diagnosis, questions distinguish among possible conditions; in troubleshooting, they isolate the source of a failure; and in legal settings, they examine competing interpretations or establish relevant facts. In each case, the agent has an objective to resolve, but the information needed to reach an answer is distributed across the conversation. This tutorial covers the foundations and recent advances in Inquisitive Conversational Agents (ICAs), bringing together research on agents that strategically control a dialogue through questioning to resolve an underlying state or answer. Unlike information-seeking systems centered on retrieving and ranking external information, ICAs acquire information through successive exchanges with a conversational counterpart. Drawing on information seeking, dialogue systems, sequential decision-making, and agentic AI, we cover methods for selecting and sequencing questions, planning over future interactions, and adapting inquiry strategies based on responses and prior interactions. We also draw on Conversational Control and Planning, Argumentation, and Evidence Attribution to examine how agents direct multi-turn interactions, reason over competing claims, and ground acquired information. Designed for researchers, students, and practitioners, the tutorial provides a unified view of this emerging area and methods for designing agents capable of sustained, goal-directed inquiry.

Presenter Bios

Victor De Lima is a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Georgetown University. His work focuses on applied large language model systems, including reinforcement learning-based optimization and agentic conversational architectures. He has published work on recommendation systems, neural re-ranking, and retrieval methods for conversational agents. Victor has substantial experience as a graduate research assistant and instructor, having taught and supported courses in deep reinforcement learning, large language models, and core computer science. His current doctoral research focuses on inquisitive conversational agents, studying how conversational systems can actively ask questions, identify missing information, and steer dialogue to support reasoning and decision-making. Victor has extensive hands-on experience fine-tuning and adapting large language models for downstream tasks, mainly for agent-based conversational systems. Through teaching computer science courses and presenting research in talks and poster sessions, he has broad experience explaining technical material to audiences with varying levels of technical background.

Website: https://www.victordelima.com/

Dr. Grace Hui Yang is Professor of Computer Science at Georgetown University, Washington D.C., U.S., leading the InfoSense (Information Retrieval and Sense-Making) group. Dr. Yang obtained her Ph.D. from Carnegie Mellon University in 2011. Her current research interests include conversational agents, deep reinforcement learning, and information retrieval. Dr. Yang is a recipient of the U.S. National Science Foundation Faculty Early Career Development Program (CAREER) Award. She has served on the organizing or program committees in recent conferences, such as SIGIR, ECIR, ACL, AAAI, ICTIR, CIKM, WSDM, and WWW, and on the editorial boards of ACM TOIS (2022-) and Information Retrieval Journal (2014-2017). She is general co-chair of SIGIR 2024. Dr. Yang has experience giving tutorials in the IR conferences – she delivered tutorials on Dynamic Information Retrieval at SIGIR’14 and WSDM’15, on Privacy-Preserving IR at ICTIR’17 and WSDM’18, and on Proactive Conversational Agents at WSDM’23 and SIGIR’23. Dr. Yang also taught in the first ACM SIGIR AFIRM Summer School in 2019.

Website: https://infosense.cs.georgetown.edu/grace

Dr. Lynda Tamine is Professor of Computer Science at Toulouse University in France. Dr. Tamine obtained her Ph.D. from Toulouse University in 2000. She was an early member of the steering committee of the French IR association ARIA (2008-2016), and she co-founded in 2016 the IAFA (Artificial Intelligence: Foundations and Applications) training program. She mainly works on neural approaches for interactive IR and knowledge-graph enhanced representation learning. She has published extensively in leading IR journals and conferences, including ACM Computing Surveys, ACM TOIS, IP&M, SIGIR, ECIR, WSDM, and CIKM, and she is currently on the editorial board of FouNdations and Trends in Information Retrieval (FNTIR). Dr Tamine has delivered tutorials on collaborative IR (ICTIR’16, ECIR’18) and semantic search for health (ECIR’18, AIME’19).

Website: https://www.irit.fr/~Lynda.Tamine-Lechani/
